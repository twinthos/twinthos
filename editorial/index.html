01 · The gap

The tools are there. Someone still has to carry the work through.

A business runs on a dozen systems that each hold one piece of the truth. The work moves when a person joins those pieces by hand: reading the enquiry, opening the record, checking the diary, sending the reply.

  • 01
    An enquiry arrives in one tool and the record that answers it lives in another.
  • 02
    Missing information stops the job halfway, and nothing restarts it on its own.
  • 03
    The follow-up that closes the loop is the first thing dropped when the week gets busy.

Each gap is small. Together they are the coordination cost nobody puts on a job description.

04 · Across time

It remembers where the work stopped.

Most work is not finished in one sitting. It waits for a reply, a document, a signature, a decision. The request keeps its identity, its history and its next step while it waits.

Thursday, 17:02

REQ-2291 is waiting on one detail: the tenant’s preferred viewing time. Nothing else in the task moves.

Next morning, 08:14

The reply arrives. The same request resumes at the diary step, not at the beginning, and the record carries the new time.

A task that restarts from the top every morning is not a colleague. Continuity is the difference between automation and responsibility.

05 · Where you decide

Your rules. Your decisions. Work moving around them.

Routine work carries on inside the permissions you set. Anything outside them stops at a named person, with the reason attached, while everything else keeps running.

Continues within your rules

  • Replying to a standard enquiry
  • Booking a viewing in open diary slots
  • Updating a record it already owns
  • Chasing a missing document once

Comes to a person

  • A discount, refund or goodwill payment
  • A contract term or price change
  • Anything outside the agreed scope
  • Two instructions that contradict each other

The system does not stop while it waits for you. Only the exception waits.
