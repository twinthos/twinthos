About

Built for people whose business asks too much of their attention.

A business creates hundreds of small commitments: reply to the enquiry, confirm the appointment, update the record, send the reminder.

Each one looks manageable. Together, they can fill the space around the work you actually want to do.

Twinthos exists to take responsibility for agreed operational work and keep it moving. We start by understanding the business, then build and manage Non-Human Intelligence around its tools, standards and boundaries.

The ambition is simple: a business that stays attentive without demanding your constant attention.

Who operates it

One person. Not a platform.

Twinthos is operated by Tyson Architect, who designs, configures and manages the workflows, monitors performance and handles issues directly.

What that means in practice

The person who designed your workflow is the person who runs it. There's no account manager between you and the work, no support queue, no handoff to a team that doesn't know your setup.

What we're not
Not a software vendor

We don't sell you a tool to manage yourself. We take on the work and manage it. You don't get another dashboard to check.

Not a consultancy

We don't deliver a report and leave. We build the workflow, run it, and stay responsible for it.

Not autonomous in the abstract

Everything the agent does is within rules agreed in writing. It doesn't "learn" beyond what we configure. It doesn't take actions outside its scope.

Why it exists

Twinthos was built because the work a business runs on — the following up, the updating, the checking, the moving of information — was never anyone's actual job. It was just the cost of having an operation.

Every business collects small commitments. A message to follow up. A record to update. A detail to check. A piece of information someone should have but doesn't. Individually, each one is small enough to dismiss. Together they fill the space around the work people were actually hired to do — and someone has to do them anyway, every time, or the operation stops paying attention.

That's operational drag. It's not a software problem. It's not a people problem. It's the gap between what the business needs and what anyone has time for — and it compounds until the people doing the real work are spending their time keeping the operation alive instead of doing it.
