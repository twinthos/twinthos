Security & control

What the workflow can read.
What it can change.
What remains with you.

A Twinthos deployment is built around clear boundaries. We define what the workflow is allowed to do before anything is activated, and we document it with you.

01

Access

What the workflow can read.

We identify the information the workflow needs to do its job, and only that information. Before activation, we document with you exactly what the workflow can access, where that information lives, and which systems provide it.

02

Authority

What the workflow can change.

The workflow can only take actions that have been approved in advance. We define what it is allowed to update, send, create or modify — and what it is not allowed to touch. Anything outside those rules stays under human control.

03

Approval

What requires a human.

Some actions are too important to automate without review. We identify the actions, spending limits and exceptions that must wait for a person before they happen, and we build the escalation into the workflow.

04

Failure

What happens when it cannot continue.

When something is outside the agreed rules, or the workflow cannot continue safely, it pauses and escalates. It does not guess. It does not force a result. A human is notified and the situation is handed back.

05

Auditability

What actions are recorded.

Every action the workflow takes is recorded. You can see what it read, what it changed, what it sent, and when. The record is available for review as part of the managed service.

06

Offboarding

How access is revoked.

If the relationship ends, the access the workflow was granted is revoked. The documentation of what was connected, what it could read, and what it could change is handed over so the boundaries are clear.

In plain language

Twinthos does not have unrestricted access to your systems.

It can only do what has been agreed in the specific workflow it is running.

It escalates when something is outside those rules.

And if you stop using it, the access ends.
