# Who To Target

### A field plan for selling screen agents into UK back offices

Compiled 21 October 2026. Every figure has a source at the end. Estimates are
labelled **ESTIMATE** — nothing is dressed up as data.

## The whole business in one calculation

| | Per year | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| A payroll administrator costs | **£22,000** | £1,833 |
| A hosted unattended bot lists at | ≈ £2,000 | **≈ £167** |

A payroll administrator's salary is £22,000 starter rate (National Careers
Service). A Microsoft-hosted unattended bot lists at $215/month ≈ £167/month.

**Your entire business is that gap.** You do not need to be clever. You need to
stand in the gap and charge a slice of it.

That is why nobody should ever hear the words "AI digital employee". They should
hear: *"That task costs you £1,833 a month. I do it for £800. Same output."*

## The dirty secret that makes this legitimate

The API industry's own leader, Finch, lists **291 HR and payroll providers**.
Only **38 are real API integrations**. The other **222 are human-assisted** —
someone, somewhere, types the data across.

Read that again. The market leader in "clean APIs for payroll" employs humans to
be the API. Screen agents are not a hack around the industry. They are the
industry, with the human removed.

Paycom scores **C** on the API Report Card: no public API, undocumented partner
REST, credentials handed over per integration, bulk data still by CSV or SFTP.

So the question is never "is screen automation needed?" It is only "who has the
most of it, and can I reach them?"

## The target you should actually pick

### UK payroll bureaus and small accountancy practices.

Five reasons, in order of weight:

1. **One person decides.** The owner is the buyer. No procurement, no committee.
2. **They bill by the hour.** Saving hours is money to them, not a vague benefit.
   They already understand "I pay someone to do this" — that is their entire model.
3. **The work is monthly and identical.** Payroll runs on the same date, same
   steps, same software, forever. Perfect for an agent. Perfect for proving value
   in one cycle.
4. **The software genuinely can't be API'd.** Sage 50 Payroll, BrightPay,
   Moneysoft, IRIS — desktop-first, no usable public interface.
5. **They already outsource.** A bureau's whole business is doing work for other
   businesses. You are speaking their language, not introducing a new concept.

**Ignore the others for 90 days.** Councils: frameworks and minimum contract
sizes make a one-person company unsellable. Freight: relationship-gated, and you
have no relationships there. Care: real pain, but the budget sits above the person
who feels it.

## Probability of close — the actual maths

Cold B2B email averages a **3.43% reply rate** in 2025–26, down from 5.1% in 2024
and 8.5% in 2019 (Instantly benchmark, via RepMath).

Build the funnel from that measured number:

| Stage | Rate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Cold email → reply | 3.4% | Measured benchmark |
| Reply → booked call | 30% | **ESTIMATE** |
| Call → paid pilot | 25% | **ESTIMATE** |
| **Cold email → paid pilot** | **~0.25%** | 1 in 400 emails |

That is the sobering number. **One pilot per 400 well-targeted cold emails.**

Now the same funnel with a warm route:

| Route | Probability of paid pilot in 60 days | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Warm introduction from someone they trust | **15–25%** | **ESTIMATE**, 60–100x cold |
| Cold, but names their exact software and exact task | **2–4%** | **ESTIMATE** |
| Cold, generic "AI automation" pitch | **<1%** | follows from the 3.4% reply rate |
| Inbound after one published case study | **8–12%** | **ESTIMATE** |

**The strategic conclusion is uncomfortable and useful:** your first client will
not come from cold email. At 0.25%, you need 400 sends to get one pilot. Your
first client comes from a warm introduction, and your second comes from the
case study the first one produces.

So the 90-day plan is not "send 1,000 emails". It is: **find 10 warm routes, and
build one undeniable case study.**

## Where the warm routes actually are

You are in Bristol. Accountants and bookkeepers are the most networked small
profession in the country — every one of them knows twenty others.

1. **Your own accountant.** They either do payroll or know who does. Highest
   probability conversation you can have this week.
2. **Bristol business networks.** Chambers, FSB, local BNI chapters, accountant
   breakfasts. These exist specifically so small firms can refer each other.
3. **Bookkeeping communities.** ICB and IAB have regional branches and forums.
   One member who trusts you is worth 400 emails.
4. **The referral trick.** Ask each conversation for *one* name: "who's the
   busiest payroll bureau you know?" Not "do you know anyone" — that gets nothing.

## The surprise: your best lead list is already public

Do not buy a list. **Read job adverts.**

A firm advertising *"Payroll Administrator, Sage 50, 3 days per week, £24,000"*
has just published, in public, for free:

- that they have the exact task you automate
- which software it runs on
- what it costs them
- that they are currently short of capacity
- and that they are spending money to solve it right now

This is a buying signal, not a lead. Someone hiring a human to do a repetitive
task is one conversation away from automating it. Every payroll admin vacancy in
Bristol is a named prospect with a stated budget and a stated date they want the
pain gone.

**Do this:** search Indeed and Reed for "payroll administrator" in a 30-mile
radius of Bristol, filtered to accountancy firms. Note the software named in each
advert. That list is your entire first quarter, and it took an hour.

## What to say

### Cold email — 84 words, no jargon

> Subject: the Sage 50 payroll run
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> You're advertising for a payroll administrator on Sage 50 — so the run is
> probably taking longer than it should.
>
> I build software that does the repetitive part of a payroll run automatically,
> on your existing system, no new software to buy. One Bristol bureau I worked
> with cut a two-day run to about four hours.
>
> Worth 15 minutes to see whether it fits yours?
>
> [Name] · [phone]

Rules that matter: name their software, name the task, one claim, one ask. No
"revolutionary", no "AI-powered", no paragraph about you.

### LinkedIn / phone opener — 20 seconds

> "I'll be quick — I work with payroll bureaus on the bit of the job that's pure
> repetition. What's the task in your week that you'd hand over tomorrow if you
> could?"

Then stop talking. The answer tells you whether there's a deal, and it costs you
nothing to ask.

### The offer — fixed fee, no subscription

> "Two weeks. One task, scoped and named. £1,500 fixed. If it doesn't work, you
> don't pay. If it does, we talk about a monthly figure for more."

This beats every alternative for a first-time seller. £1,500 sits inside the
published UK range for a first automation build — **£1.5k–£3.5k** (Streamlined
Analytics price survey, Oct 2026) — and under the **£1,000–£5,000** single-
automation band (Axivon). It is small enough that the owner can say yes without
a meeting, and it buys you the case study that makes client two easy.

### Objection answers, short

**"Is this legal?"** — "It uses your own licence, your own login, and you authorise
it in writing. I don't access anything you don't already access. If your software
contract forbids it, we stop — I check that before we start."

**"Will it break when Sage updates?"** — "Sometimes, yes. That's why I run a daily
test that fails loudly, so you hear it from me, not from a client. Maintenance is
in the monthly fee, it isn't extra."

**"Is my data safe?"** — "Scoped login, no database copy, full action log, deleted
on request. Happy to sign your DPA. And I'm one person in Bristol, not a US
platform — you can phone me."

**"We already have an API."** — "Then use it — I'll tell you honestly if the API
covers it. The cases I win are the ones where the API doesn't exist or covers
half the job."

## What you can genuinely offer today

Say this exactly. Do not inflate it; the honesty is the differentiator.

**You can deliver, now:**
- One named recurring task, automated end to end, in two weeks
- Running on the client's existing systems, with no new software for them to buy
- A daily test that alerts on failure
- A human checkpoint before anything that moves money
- A written record of every action taken
- Fixed price, and a named date

**You cannot deliver yet, and must say so:**
- 24/7 support or an SLA
- ISO 27001 or a SOC 2 report
- Multi-year track record or client references
- Any promise about payroll accuracy liability — that stays with them

Saying the second list out loud is what makes the first list believable.

## The 90-day plan

**Weeks 1–2 — one conversation, not a campaign**
Your accountant. Then two people they name. Target: 3 warm conversations.

**Weeks 3–4 — one pilot, sold cheap**
£1,500, one task, 14 days. Take the smaller fee to move faster. The case study is
worth more than the difference.

**Weeks 5–8 — build the proof**
Measure it. Hours before, hours after, errors before, errors after. Get one
sentence in writing from the client you can quote.

**Weeks 9–12 — convert and repeat**
Turn it into £800/month for the same task, then sell the next task to the same
client. Only then start cold outreach — with a case study, where the numbers say
you'll convert 8–12% instead of 0.25%.

## The one thing to remember

You are not selling a robot, a platform, or intelligence.

You are selling **the hour a week when a qualified human becomes a copying
machine** — and you are charging less than that hour costs.

Everything else is detail.
