# Twinthos image doctrine (21 October 2026)

## What the research says

- A recognisably synthetic image is one of the loudest "this page was not made by
  people" tells. A Raptive study of 3,000 US adults found content people suspected
  was AI rated roughly half as trustworthy; Meltwater/YouGov (n≈10,000) found 32%
  would trust a brand less. The problem is generic AI imagery, not photography.
- The brain processes an image in about 13ms, before a word is read. The image is
  the first judgement of the article.
- Average time on a blog article is about 52 seconds and readers consume roughly
  28% of the words. So an article needs to be short, and the image has to do work
  in that first glance.

## What our images must do

1. **Show the thing the article is about.** A phone, a folder, a cash machine, a
   reception desk. Not a symbol of it. If the caption cannot be written literally
   ("a stack of invoices on a desk"), the image is wrong.
2. **Look documented, not designed.** One subject, real daylight or one lamp,
   slightly off-centre, a bit of mess. The reader should think "that is my office",
   not "that is a stock photo".
3. **Be British and specific.** Lever-arch files, A4 paper, a beige carpet tile, a
   windowsill kettle, a high-street cash machine, a card reader on a counter. Local
   detail is what makes a photograph read as real.
4. **Colour, not monochrome mood.** Grey concrete halls and empty black rooms were
   the old style and they communicated nothing. Real offices are beige, blue-grey
   and fluorescent, with one warm lamp.
5. **No metaphor.** Banned: glowing brains, circuit boards shaped like heads,
   robots shaking hands, floating holograms, neon data streams, chess pieces,
   labyrinth mazes, empty minimal architecture, people staring at light.

## Prompt recipe

`Photograph, taken on a phone or a 35mm camera, of <one concrete subject> in <a
specific real place>. Natural window light, slightly cluttered, ordinary British
office detail. Shallow depth of field, no people posing, no text, no logos, no
brand names. Documentary style, colour, fine grain.`

Ten words of prohibition at the end of every prompt: no text, no logos, no
watermark, no people's faces, no hands, no robots, no glowing elements, no
holograms, no surreal composition, no stock-photo smiling.

## Caption rule

Every image carries a caption that states what it is, in six words or fewer.

## Test before publishing

Look at the image for three seconds, then answer: what is in it? If the answer is
"a mood", delete it. If the answer is "a stack of paper next to a phone on a real
desk", publish it.
