# Twinthos Notes — house voice (2026)

Written for one reader: an owner or operations director of a UK business with
5 to 300 staff. They are skimming on a phone, between meetings. They do not
know what a token, an agent, a workflow engine or a context window is. They
do know what a payroll run costs them.

## The test every paragraph must pass

Read it aloud. If a smart 14-year-old would ask "what does that mean?", rewrite
it. If you would not say it to a customer across a desk, delete it.

## Hard rules

1. **Title: 62 characters or fewer.** Name the thing the reader would type or
   say. No colon-and-clause constructions, no "including the three lines
   suppliers leave out". Bad: "The three-year cost of a digital employee,
   including the three lines suppliers leave out." Good: "What a digital
   employee really costs over three years".
2. **First 45 words answer the question in the title.** No throat-clearing, no
   scene-setting, no "in today's landscape". State the answer, then earn it.
3. **700 to 950 words. Total.** Not 2,000. Not 4,900. If a section cannot
   survive the cut, it was not needed.
4. **Sentences average under 18 words.** One idea per paragraph. Paragraphs of
   one to three sentences are fine and encouraged.
5. **Second person.** "You", "your team". Never "businesses should consider".
6. **One number per section, in the sentence.** Numbers come from the existing
   article or its sources. Do not invent, round differently, or add a statistic
   that is not already in the source article.
7. **No jargon without an immediate plain gloss in the same sentence.** Banned
   unless quoted and explained: agentic, orchestration, paradigm, leverage,
   synergy, operationalise, scalable solution, transformation journey, robust,
   seamless, cutting-edge, harness, unlock, empower, ecosystem, best-in-class.
8. **Headings are claims, not labels.** Bad: "Understanding the cost model."
   Good: "The fee is the small part".
9. **No em dashes.** Use full stops. Occasional colon. Never "—".
10. **Say what it costs, who does the work, and what happens when it goes
    wrong.** Those three are why the reader is here.

## Shape that works

- Title (<=62 chars)
- 40 to 50 word answer block, bolded lead in the template
- 4 to 6 H2 sections, each 100 to 160 words
- One table or list of real numbers
- One short section headed "What this means for you" or equivalent, 60 words
- Optional FAQ of 3 questions, answered in 2 sentences each

## Voice examples

Bad: "Effective deployment of an AI agent requires consideration of operational
governance frameworks."
Good: "Someone has to sign off what it does. Make that one person, by name."

Bad: "Digital employees represent a paradigm shift in workforce scalability."
Good: "A digital employee does one job. You can hire a second one when the first
one is busy."

Bad: "Costs can vary depending on implementation complexity."
Good: "Setup is £2,500. The monthly fee is £750. Everything else is either your
time or your software."

## What you must preserve

Keep the factual claims and their sources that already exist in the article you
are rewriting. If the old article cites a report, a date and a number, keep
them and keep the link text. Cut everything else. If a claim looks unverifiable,
drop it rather than soften it.
