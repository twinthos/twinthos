Not which model.

Definition

Eight things a brain has to have.

This is the working definition, in plain English. If one of these is missing, the system does not stop, it fills the gap with a guess.

  • IdentityWho is asking, what they are allowed to see, and which company the answer belongs to. One client's knowledge never reaches another's.
  • Authoritative factsThe agreed answers your business has signed off: the policy in force today, the current price list, the procedure that applies now.
  • Knowledge libraryYour documents and records held as evidence, so every answer can point to where it came from.
  • Persistent memoryDecisions, preferences and corrections that survive the conversation, the week and the staff change.
  • ProjectsEach piece of work with its scope, state, owner and next step, kept current by whoever is carrying it.
  • Relationship graphHow your people, clients, suppliers, sites and products connect, so "who handles this" has an answer.
  • RetrievalFinding the right record and returning it with its evidence and the timestamp it was true at, so answers age visibly.
  • GovernancePermissions, approvals and a log of what was asked, what was done and what was sent to a person. Nothing happens silently.

Memory must be able to say I don't know. A brain full of unverified claims makes a business less reliable, not more.

It replaces "I am not sure" with a confident wrong answer, and it is worse than having no brain at all. In a Twinthos Brain, everything is marked as verified, draft, or not held, and when there is no record, the answer says so.

Memory hierarchy

Four layers, slowest at the bottom.

Not everything worth knowing should be remembered in the same way. Each layer has a different lifetime, a different owner and a different rule, which is what stops a memory system becoming a hoard.

01

Current working context

What is happening right now: the task in hand, the documents open, this week's instructions. Short-lived by design, so today's work does not harden into tomorrow's policy.

02

Persistent memories

Approved decisions, agreed preferences and corrections that should outlive the session, the things a good colleague would not make you explain twice.

03

Organisational knowledge

Policies, procedures, project history and reference material owned by the company, version-controlled and attributed to a source. This layer changes only when somebody with authority changes it.

04

External source systems

Your email, calendar, CRM, accounting software and file store. Twinthos reads these when asked rather than keeping a copy that goes stale, so the answer reflects the record as it stands today.

Built to survive change

Nothing here depends on one model, one machine or one employee.

The agent, the knowledge and the computer are not inseparably bound. The Brain survives a model change, a computer failure, or replacing one digital employee with another. Each piece can be replaced without rebuilding the others, so nothing you have taught the Brain walks out with it.

  • The model changesA brain is not a model. A new one can run behind the same knowledge, permissions and history, so your memory does not reset when a vendor ships an update.
  • The computer failsThe knowledge belongs to your company, not to one machine. A replacement is provisioned against the same Brain and the same rules.
  • The digital employee changesSwap one digital employee for another and the replacement inherits the memory, the open projects and the Authority Line. There is no handover week.

The Authority Line

Below the line it acts. Above it, your people decide.

Below the Authority Line, digital employees act: sending routine email, updating records, booking appointments and requesting documents. Above it, a human decides: agreeing a price reduction, issuing a refund, approving a contract change, committing company funds. The line is drawn with you, in writing, per client, it is not a default we ship.

The digital employee acts

A human decides

A digital employee frees your people to be human. The recurring work - chasing, logging, confirming, updating, following up - is what a person currently does to keep a process alive, and it stops being their job.

What is left for your people is judgement, relationships and decisions, the part that needed a human anyway. Work handled. Time returned.
