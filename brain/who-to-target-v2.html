The decision07

Target: UK payroll bureaus.

30,000 to 40,000 firms. One person decides. They bill by the hour, so saved hours are money. The work is identical every month. And the software cannot be API’d.

The sentence that opens the door

“You already sell hours to your clients. I sell hours back to you.”

08
Why they say yes08

Five reasons, in order of weight.

  1. One person decides. The owner is the buyer.
  2. They bill by the hour. Saved hours are cash.
  3. Monthly and identical. Provable in one cycle.
  4. No API exists. Sage 50, BrightPay, IRIS.
  5. They already outsource. Selling work is their model.

The conclusion12

Your first client will be warm.

At one in four hundred, cold email cannot produce your first deal. It can only produce your tenth.

So the 90 days are not a campaign. They are ten warm routes and one undeniable case study.

13
Where the doors are13

Four conversations, not four hundred emails.

  1. Your own accountant. They run payroll or know who does.
  2. Whoever does your books. Same door, different key.
  3. Bristol networks. Chambers, FSB, BNI breakfasts.
  4. Bookkeeping bodies. ICB and IAB branches.

“Who’s the busiest payroll bureau you know?”

Never ask “do you know anyone”. Ask for a specific person. A specific question produces a name.

14
The surprise14

Don’t buy a list. Read job adverts.

Payroll Administrator Sage 50 · 3 days/week · £24,000 Bristol · accountancy practice

That advert publishes, for free: the task, the software, the budget, the shortfall — and the fact that they are spending money on it right now.

This is not a lead. It is a buying signal.

Every payroll vacancy in Bristol is a named prospect with a stated budget.

What to say15

The cold email.

Subject: the Sage 50 payroll run Hi [Name], You're advertising for a payroll administrator on Sage 50 — so the run is probably taking longer than it should. I build software that does the repetitive part of a payroll run automatically, on your existing system. Worth 15 minutes? [Name] · [phone]

Name their software. Name the task. One claim. One ask. Delete any sentence that begins “We are a…”

The four questions18

Answered in one breath.

“Is this legal?”
Your licence, your login, your written authorisation. If the contract forbids it, we stop.

“Will it break?”
Sometimes. A daily test fails loudly, so you hear it from me.

“Is my data safe?”
Scoped login, no copy, full log, deleted on request.

“We have an API.”
Then use it — I’ll tell you honestly if it covers this.

The pattern

Every answer concedes something real, then offers a control.

What you actually have19

The two lists.

You can deliver

One named task, automated end to end, in two weeks. Their systems. A daily test. A human checkpoint on money. A written log. Fixed price. Named date.

You cannot — say so

An SLA. ISO 27001. A track record. Any promise about payroll accuracy liability.

Every competitor overclaims. Naming your own limits is the cheapest trust you will ever buy.
