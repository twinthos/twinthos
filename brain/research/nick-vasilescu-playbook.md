# The Nick Vasilescu playbook — what he does, quoted

Source set collected 21 October 2026: **44 posts** from his X timeline, **16
published notes** from nickvasilescu.com, and **one full interview transcript**
(YouTube, CGkC8ukXnfw). Every quote below links to the post it came from.

He is the co-founder of Orgo (cloud computers for AI agents, YC-backed) and he
was running an agency selling AI employees before that. He is the closest thing
to a public operator's manual for the business Twinthos is in. Treat him as the
field leader: he is three years ahead on delivery and one year ahead on price.

---

## 1. Sell one job. The category kills the sale.

> "selling an AI agent that does everything makes zero sense. nobody buys it.
> ask the operator one question: what manual workflow would you pay first to get
> rid of?"

— [20 Oct 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2112333064609190104)

> "people don't want an agent. they want their problem solved."

— [13 Aug 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2087918771574899148)

**Against us:** Twinthos sells "digital employees" and a "company brain". Those
are categories. A category is not a job, and nobody can sign for a category.
Every document should name one job before it names the company.

## 2. The boring work is where the money is.

> "he's deploying computer-use agents that log into the client's actual software
> and do the repetitive stuff their office manager used to do. invoice
> processing, appointment scheduling, follow-ups. no one on twitter is talking
> about this because it's boring. that's exactly why it works. boring businesses
> have budgets and they don't churn."

— [21 Aug 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2090818762903626173)

**For us:** this is the entire argument for the eight trades in the target book.
Nobody is posting about bordereaux or funding returns. That is the advantage.

## 3. Start with people who already know you.

> "a solo web designer in Belgium started offering agent setups to his existing
> clients. €2,750 per install. €267/mo to manage. five booked for september.
> zero competition in his entire country."

— [30 Aug 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2094078436620804114)

His longer note adds the reason: the customers were local business owners who
already followed his work, in their own language.
([note](https://www.nickvasilescu.com/blog/your-next-agent-customer-may-already-know-you))

**Against us:** the 114-firm cold list is the slow path. The fast path is
everyone Tyson has already built something for. HotBox is a prospect, not a
client, and it has been one for months.

## 4. Price: paid install, modest monthly.

> "2 ways i've seen work: 1. high ticket upfront ($15K+) ... then downsell into a
> $2-3K/mo retainer after 2-3 months ... 2. flat monthly retainer ($5K/mo) for
> simplicity. this is what we do."

— [11 Aug 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2087218300321398805)

And his own first sale, from the interview:

> "by the end of the first week of starting this idea, I sold my first client,
> $3,500 for an agent."

— [interview, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CGkC8ukXnfw)

| Model | Up front | Monthly | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nick's first agent | $3,500 | n/a | interview |
| Belgian designer | €2,750 | €267 | post, 30 Aug |
| Nick's option 1 | $15,000+ | $2,000–3,000 | post, 11 Aug |
| Nick's option 2 | none | $5,000 flat | post, 11 Aug |

**Against us:** `company/pricing.md` has a £2,500 setup and a £5,000 monthly.
The evidence says the monthly is too high for one role and the setup is too low
for the work. £3,500 install plus £1,250 a month sits inside both ends.

## 5. The SOP is the product. Capturing it is the hard part.

> "capturing a client's workflow into an SOP is harder than building the agent
> itself. clients expect employees to figure things out intuitively. agents
> can't. they need every step documented. the SOP is the real bottleneck in
> every agent deployment."

— [22 Aug 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2091178577702129964)

**For us:** this is the argument for charging for the audit. The £999 Operations
Audit is not a lead magnet — it is the part of the job that decides whether the
deployment works. Price it like work, not like marketing.

## 6. The agent is the delivery mechanism. You are the product.

> "managed agents are a consulting business disguised as a tech product. my
> expertise gets encoded into an agent that runs 24/7 across 20 clients ... the
> agent is the delivery mechanism. you are the product."

— [13 Aug 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2087979246798999651)

> "a company brain also needs an update process. decide which sources belong in
> it, who can access them, and who is responsible for keeping them current."

— [note, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.nickvasilescu.com/blog/company-brains-as-a-service)

**For us:** this is not a criticism of Twinthos; it is a description of it. Sell
the accountability, not the software. The monthly fee buys someone who answers
the phone when it breaks.

## 7. Meet them where they already are.

> "an AI employee should have a business phone. your client saves a contact.
> texts it on iMessage. sends it the files it needs."

— [14 Sep 2026](https://x.com/nickvasilescu/status/2099572533569913292)

**Against us:** a Twinthos employee currently has no address. No email, no
number. The demo is a document. Give the demo employee its own mailbox and let
the prospect send it work — the mail plumbing is already installed on this
machine.

## 8. Long form sells. Short form only gets attention.

From the interview: he has stopped optimising for viral short videos because
they do not convert, and moved to a podcast plus long conversations, two or
three a week, zero paid acquisition.

**For us:** Tyson's publishing should be the job, not the announcement. One post
per week showing a real job being done, with the numbers.

## 9. The five-year bet, in his words

> "if you're thinking five years out, you're probably thinking about building AI
> digital workforce ... you probably want to be building a vertical specialized
> AI worker who's really good at particular domain."

— [interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CGkC8ukXnfw)

## What to take, in order

1. **Name the job on every page.** Not "digital employee" — "the post-completion
   desk", "the renewal desk", "the claim chase".
2. **Price the install properly.** £3,500, paid, before any monthly.
3. **Sell to people who already know you first.** Then the list.
4. **Give the demo an address.** Email or number, live, before the meeting.
5. **Charge for the SOP work.** The audit is the product's first half.
6. **Publish the job, weekly.** Not the company.

## Where he is not a guide

- His numbers come from businesses buying one cheap install, not from
  mid-market UK firms with compliance officers.
- He sells infrastructure. Infrastructure is a horizontal business with a
  developer audience; Twinthos sells labour, which is a local, trust-based
  business. The pricing transfers, the go-to-market does not.
- His content engine works because he has 100k+ followers and a YC badge. Do
  not plan on distribution you do not have yet.
