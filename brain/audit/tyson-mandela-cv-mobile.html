What I am for

Most automation projects die in the demo. Mine are specified like a job description: what the agent receives, what it decides alone, what it escalates, and what evidence it leaves behind. That is the difference between a clever prototype and a thing a business will let run on a Tuesday morning when nobody is watching.

How I work

Four rules I do not break

  • Truth before persuasion. If a number cannot be sourced, it does not go in the document. I would rather hand over a short verified list than a long plausible one.
  • Evidence before the claim. Every delivery carries its own proof: the page that was opened, the date it was checked, the file that shows the work.
  • Human oversight for anything consequential. Agents propose; a person approves anything that spends money, contacts a customer or changes a record.
  • Finished, not started. A build is done when it has run against live data, not when the code compiles.

An AI operations or automation lead role where the work is real: repeatable processes, measurable hours returned, and a team that wants the system to survive contact with a Monday.
