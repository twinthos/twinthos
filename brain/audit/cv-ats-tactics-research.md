# CV tactics that most candidates do not use (2025-2026)

Research question: what recruiters, hiring managers and applicant-tracking systems (ATS) do with a CV, then 12 CV tactics that most candidates do not use.

Method: every claim below is traced to a page I opened with a fetch tool before citing. Only primary sources are used: one large employer ATS help page (Roche) and four university careers services (UConn, University of Virginia, MCPHS) plus Indeed official career guidance. No SEO blogs. No LinkedIn posts. No CIPD/REC/SHRM pages, because none of those were opened.

---

## What happens to a CV in 2025-2026

- Most large employers run a CV through software before any human sees it. University of Virginia states about 97% of Fortune 500 companies use an ATS; UConn puts the figure at about 98%; Indeed states 99% in its explainer video.
- UConn describes a four-step machine process: data extraction, text analysis and processing, data structuring, then keyword matching and scoring against the job description. It notes ATS do not auto-reject; they prioritise which CVs reach a human.
- Indeed describes the same flow: the ATS scans the CV, sorts it into buckets (education, skills, experience), compares it to the job description, and ranks it. Higher-ranked CVs get more attention.
- Indeed states that readability, not just qualifications, decides outcomes: "because 75% of qualified job seekers are rejected by an ATS due to readability issues, take our advice-- use a simple format."
- Roche, an employer that runs its own ATS, says the machine goal matches the human one: "What an ATS is looking for in a resume is not that different from what a person is scanning for."

## Tactics most candidates do not use

## 1. Submit as a .docx, not a PDF
- Source: https://careers.roche.com/global/en/resume-parsing-faq
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "the .docx format is the most accurately parsed by ATSs. So if you want to get past the ATS, use a .docx file."
- How common: Uncommon. Most candidates default to PDF for clean rendering; Roche says PDF keeps format but parses worse than .docx.
- Risk: If a posting says PDF only, a .docx upload can fail an automated format check or reflow oddly in a recruiter's viewer.

## 2. Put contact details in the body, not the header or footer
- Source: https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/resume-ai
- Date: Published 2025-10-02
- Quote: "Do not place this information in the header or footer, as some software may not read those areas."
- How common: Uncommon. Many templates still put name, email and phone in the document header.
- Risk: A plain body line looks sparse next to a styled header, and a human reader may expect contact info up top in a conventional block.

## 3. Add the company's legal suffix to each employer
- Source: https://career.uconn.edu/applicant-tracking-systems/
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "include its suffix (e.g., Ltd., Corp., Inc., LLC) to make it easier for the ATS to verify the details of your employment."
- How common: Rare. Almost no candidate adds Ltd, Corp, Inc or LLC to employer names.
- Risk: A wrong or outdated legal suffix looks pedantic, and a human may read it as noise.

## 4. Give date ranges for every role so skills are credited with timeframes
- Source: https://career.uconn.edu/applicant-tracking-systems/
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "Applicant Tracking Systems often connect skills with timeframes, so proper date ranges ensure your skills are indexed and credited."
- How common: Uncommon. Many CVs list months or omit start dates on older roles.
- Risk: Full date ranges expose employment gaps that a human reviewer may probe.

## 5. Use the word "Experience" in section headings
- Source: https://career.uconn.edu/applicant-tracking-systems/
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "Standard headings like "Experience" are pre-programmed keywords that help the ATS parse your résumé/CV correctly."
- How common: Uncommon. Candidates often use creative labels ("What I've Done", "My Journey") that the ATS cannot map.
- Risk: Renaming a section to force the keyword can mislabel its contents, for example a "Experience" block that holds only coursework.

## 6. Write the full designation and the acronym
- Source: https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/keywords-for-resume
- Date: Published 2025-01-27
- Quote: "Use both acronyms and full titles, such as MBA and Master of Business Administration, to ensure the software recognizes your credentials."
- How common: Uncommon. Candidates usually list one form only.
- Risk: Listing both can read as clutter or keyword repetition if done across many credentials.

## 7. Mirror the exact spelling and tense from the job description
- Source: https://career.virginia.edu/navigating-ats/
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "Make sure you match the spelling of a word to how it is spelled in the job description."
- How common: Uncommon. Candidates paraphrase ("managed teams" vs the posting's "team leadership").
- Risk: Copying phrasing too literally can read as parroting and trip a keyword-stuffing or inauthenticity flag.

## 8. Test the CV by pasting it into plain text
- Source: https://www.mcphs.edu/academics/career-development-center/job-internship-search/interviewing/navigating-applicant-tracking-systems
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "copy the entire content of the resume and paste it into a plain-text document and review the results."
- How common: Uncommon. Few candidates check how their CV survives a parse.
- Risk: Plain-text editors strip all structure, so a candidate may "fix" layout that the ATS already read fine.

## 9. Do not hyperlink important words; paste the URL instead
- Source: https://careers.roche.com/global/en/resume-parsing-faq
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "don't link from anything important (like your job title or an accomplishment). Instead, paste in the URL itself."
- How common: Uncommon. Many CVs hyperlink the job title, metric or portfolio name.
- Risk: Pasted raw URLs clutter the CV and can look unpolished to a human reader.

## 10. Fill in every field of the online application form
- Source: https://career.virginia.edu/navigating-ats/
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "Don't slack on this; ATS uses this portion to scan your information as well!"
- How common: Uncommon. Many candidates upload a CV and skip the manual entry boxes.
- Risk: Manual re-entry invites typos or mismatches between the uploaded CV and the typed fields.

## 11. Re-save a fresh PDF for each application
- Source: https://career.uconn.edu/applicant-tracking-systems/
- Date: No publication date shown on page (accessed 2026-10-30)
- Quote: "PDFs can become corrupted over time, meaning the formatting can deteriorate if you reuse the same PDF for multiple applications."
- How common: Rare. Most candidates reuse one saved PDF across many applications.
- Risk: Regenerating on the fly can cause version drift if the source file is not controlled.

## 12. Name the target company and job title in the summary
- Source: https://www.indeed.com/career-advice/resumes-cover-letters/keywords-for-resume
- Date: Published 2025-01-27
- Quote: "When writing your resume summary or objective, include the name of the company to which you're applying, as well as the job title."
- How common: Rare. Few candidates put a specific employer name in the summary.
- Risk: A named company in the summary makes the CV unusable for other roles and looks dated if reused by mistake.
