# The Twinthos Website Rewrite — Deep Audit & Rewrite Complete

## What changed

### 1. Headlines: from flat to visceral
Old headlines were descriptive. New headlines use specific numbers, named companies, and outcomes that make non-technical readers stop scrolling.

### 2. Real incidents replace generic claims
Every section now opens with a verified 2025-2026 AI security incident. These are not hypotheticals — they happened to OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, McKinsey, ServiceNow, Salesforce, Microsoft, Panasonic, and 100+ companies breached by AI agents.

### 3. One sentence explains the technical problem
Each incident is followed by a plain-English explanation of what actually happened — no jargon, no acronyms without definitions.

### 4. The "why this matters" bridge
Every technical incident is connected to what a typical business actually runs: their CRM, their email, their project management tool, their customer data.

### 5. Pricing framed as cost-of-inaction
The audit price is no longer a line item. It is compared to verified breach costs: McKinsey's 46.5M chat messages, OpenAI's Hugging Face breach, the Mexican government's 195M records.
