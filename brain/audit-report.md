# Forensic Audit — The Block Management Recommendation Is Withdrawn

**Twinthos · 21 October 2026**

Five independent reviewers took the previous recommendation apart. Three findings do not survive. This is what was wrong, and what to do instead.

---

## The verdict

**Killed: block management at £5,000 a month.** The fee exceeds the buyer's entire pre-tax profit, the incumbent shipped the product two weeks ago, and the law does not say what I said it says.

**Survives, for a different reason: recruitment.** Not because of admin volume — because of profit per employee. One number settles it, and it is in section 9.

**The deeper error:** I chose the sector by how much admin it had, not by whether the client could afford the fee.

---

## Error 1 — I priced above their profit

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| 1,000 units × £300 fee | £300,000 |
| Pre-tax profit at 10% | £30,000 |
| **My £2,500/mo fee** | **£30,000/yr** |

**The fee equals 100% of their profit**, at the smallest tier. No managing director signs that.

*Fees £150–£800/unit/yr (LevyBoard; Wilson Hawkins 2024–25). PBT margin 10–18%, median ~10% across 599 UK property-management companies (theaccounts.uk).*

---

## Error 2 — I inverted a number

I wrote: *1.23m units under ARMA members.*

The source says the opposite:

> "...just 300 are members of ARMA. That means around 1.23 million residential units are managed by **non-ARMA** firms."
> — blockmanagementuk.ltd, ArticleID=139 (2020)

1.23m is the **non-ARMA** book. The figure is also six years old, and the 4,100 firm count is a modelled estimate, not a register.

---

## Error 3 — the law does not say that

I wrote: *a managing agent can be the Principal Accountable Person.*

The accountable person is normally the freeholder, the RMC, the RTM company or the local authority. A managing agent usually acts **on behalf of** them.

> "You can delegate the work; you cannot delegate the duty."

The agent produces the evidence. The client signs it off. The "legal defensibility" pitch fails in most leasehold structures — and a competent client would correct it in the first meeting.

---

## Error 4 — the incumbent shipped it

MRI, the system of record for block management, launched **Agora Orchestrator** on **6 October 2026**. First client: James Andrew Residential, 6,500 homes, automating accounting, document processing and maintenance triage.

You would have been selling agentic automation into a market where the software vendor already gives it away, bundled, inside the platform the client already pays for.

---

## Error 5 — "same software" was 8%

**8%** of residential managing agents are on the stack I called universal: 70 ARMA members out of ~870 firms.

The whole argument rested on "one build works everywhere". Their own number says one build works at one firm in twelve.

---

## The correction

**Ask what the role earns, not what it costs.**

| Test | Right? |
|---|---|
| "How much admin do they have?" | Wrong |
| "What does that role cost?" | Wrong |
| **"How much profit per head?"** | **Right** |

Admin volume is a pain measure. Profit per head is a payment measure. Only one of them pays your invoice.

---

## The number that settles it

Hays, year to 30 June 2025: **£972.4m of net fees from 6,070 consultants** — £160,200 of fee income generated per consultant, per year.

| Sector | Per head |
|---|---|
| **Recruitment** — fees generated | **£160,200** |
| **Block management** — profit generated | ≈ £6,000 |

A £60,000 annual fee is **37%** of one recruiter's output, and **1,000%** of one block manager's profit. That is the entire audit in one comparison.

*Hays plc preliminary results FY2025. The £6,000 is my estimate (£300k revenue × 10% PBT ÷ 5 staff).*

---

## Corrected pricing — price the desk, not the person

The digital employee does not serve one recruiter. It serves a desk: 20–30 consultants all doing the same repeating tasks.

| What it covers | Fee |
|---|---|
| One workflow, one branch | £1,500 pilot |
| **A full desk, 20–30 consultants** | **£5,000/mo** |
| Multi-branch group | £1,200/branch |

£5,000/mo against 25 consultants is £200 per consultant per month — about 1.5% of the fee income each one generates. That is a defensible invoice.

---

## Direction

1. **Drop block management** — not forever, until you have a reference client.
2. **Run the profit-per-head test** on any sector before writing another word about it.
3. **Call Hurley and Hipkiss.** Both named, both reachable, both in a sector that can afford £5,000.
4. **Sell one desk, not one task.** The pilot proves it; the desk pays for it.
5. **Never cite your own pricing file as evidence again.** It is a test price, not a fact.
