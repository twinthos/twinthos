# The product, stated plainly

Canonical definition of what Twinthos sells. Written because the pitch kept drifting into
"AI assistant" language, which is the wrong category and loses the argument before it starts.
Read this before writing any offer, deck or email.

STATUS: founder-confirmed positioning (21 Oct 2026), reconciled with mission.md, services.md
and pricing.md. Prices in pricing.md remain TEST PRICES — do not quote as list.

---

## One sentence

**Twinthos sells a digital employee: one defined job, done inside the systems the client
already owns, by an agent that remembers the business and answers to a named human.**

## The three parts (all three, or it is not the product)

1. **The Company Brain.** Persistent, isolated knowledge for one company: its rules, approved
   facts, tone, past decisions, open projects, exceptions, and the permissions the employee
   may act under. It does not reset between conversations. It never invents a memory and never
   shares data across clients.
2. **The Digital Employee.** The acting layer. It holds one job with a written definition —
   inputs, outputs, escalation rule, what it must never do — and it works on triggers (a new
   email, document, instruction, call), not on someone remembering to prompt it.
3. **The Desk.** It works inside the client's existing tools through the screen, so there is no
   integration project and no API the client has to buy. A first job is live in about two hours.
   Twinthos runs it and stays accountable for it.

## What it is not — and why the distinction is structural, not marketing

| It is not | Why that matters commercially |
|---|---|
| **ChatGPT, Grok, Muse, Claude, Gemini** — any model | A model is a general assistant sold per seat to anybody. It has no persistent knowledge of *your* firm, no permissions, no defined job, no audit trail and no one on the hook when it is wrong. You buy it and the work still lands on the same person. |
| **A chatbot** | A chatbot waits to be asked and produces text. A digital employee is triggered by work arriving and produces work product: a filed document, a booked job, a chased invoice, a completed record. |
| **A copilot** | A copilot makes a person faster at what they were already doing, so the headcount stays. The employee does the thing, so the headcount is the variable. |
| **An AI wrapper or a prompt library** | The value is persistence, retrieval, permissions and verification — the brain and the guardrails. Take those away and it is a prompt in a box. |
| **A retrained model** | We make no such claim. The underlying model is a commodity input, swappable. What is not swappable is the brain, the job definition and the history. Change the model tomorrow and the employee keeps its job, its knowledge and its record. |
| **An integration project** | Screen-based agency means no API, no vendor ticket, no six-month review. This is why a first job ships in hours rather than quarters. |
| **A consultancy deliverable** | A report is read once. The employee runs every day, and the client can hand it a new task without briefing an agency again. |

**The one-line test:** if the buyer describes what they are buying as "AI", they will price it
against a £20/month subscription. If they describe it as "a person who does this job", they will
price it against a salary. The entire sale is moving them to the second description — honestly,
because that is what it is.

## What the buyer is actually paying for

- **Continuity.** Every approved fact and prior decision stays. No re-explaining the client, the
  pricing rules, the tone, the exceptions.
- **A unit of work with an owner.** The job has a name, a definition and a named human who signs
  off consequential actions.
- **Isolation.** Other clients' data is not in the same head. This is a design requirement, not
  a feature bullet.
- **Speed to value.** The first job is live in about two hours; broader scope takes longer and we
  say so.

## The money frame (arithmetic, with the assumption stated)

One UK admin: £28,000 salary × 1.25 loading (employer NIC, pension, kit — an assumption, not a
quoted rate) = **£35,000/year = £2,917/month = £17.95/hour** across 1,950 working hours.

- **Foundation, £750/month (£9,000/yr)** = 26% of one admin hire. Buys one workflow, not a role.
- **Brain + digital employee, £5,000/month (£60,000/yr)** = **1.7 admin hires**. It has to
  replace roughly two salaries, or grow revenue by more than £60,000 a year. If it cannot do
  either, the job is wrong — not the price.

Say that out loud in the room. It is the sentence that makes the number credible and it stops
the client finding the flaw for you.

## What we will not claim

- That the model has been retrained, fine-tuned or made smarter.
- A client result we have not delivered. No client has been delivered this as a paid service yet.
- A price as "list" when pricing.md marks it as a test to validate.
- That a demo is a production deployment.
