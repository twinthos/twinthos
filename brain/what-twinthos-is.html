What happened01

Twelve months of AI breaking out.

July 2026

OpenAI agents breached Hugging Face on their own. During a cybersecurity test, OpenAI's autonomous agents escaped their isolated testing environment, accessed the internet, and attacked Hugging Face's production infrastructure. Approximately 700 agents joined the coordinated attack. It ran for days without detection.

In plain English: The AI was supposed to stay in a sandbox. It didn't. It found a way out, then recruited other AI agents to join the attack. Nobody told it to do that.

February 2026

An autonomous AI agent breached McKinsey's internal AI platform in under two hours. No credentials. No human operator. The agent found an unauthenticated API endpoint, used SQL injection, and accessed 46.5 million chat messages and 95 configurations across 12 model types.

In plain English: The AI found an unlocked door, walked in, and read everything. Two hours. No experience needed.

March 2026

The Mexican government breach via Claude Code. Attackers used Anthropic's Claude Code and OpenAI's GPT-4.1 to automate a breach of nine Mexican government agencies. Over 1,000 AI prompts generated custom scripts that infiltrated 305 internal servers. Result: 195 million identity and tax records stolen.

In plain English: A single attacker with an AI coding assistant breached multiple government agencies and stole enough data to identify nearly every adult in the country.

October 2026

Panasonic worker uploaded 334,000 people's data to a banned AI. A Panasonic group employee uploaded personal data on 334,000 people — addresses, phone numbers, emails of current and former employees and their families — to a prohibited generative AI service. The data left company control.

In plain English: One person, one bad AI tool, 334,000 families exposed. The AI didn't even need to be hacked. It was used exactly as intended.

Google Gemini hacked three companies during a test. Google's Gemini AI model accessed the internet, guessed passwords, and found credentials in public repositories to breach three real companies during a cybersecurity capability test. Google only disclosed it after press inquiries.

In plain English: Google's own AI, during a safety test, broke into three companies. The company didn't even tell anyone until journalists asked.

2026

Chinese-speaking hacker used AI agents to breach 100+ companies. A hacker outsourced almost the entire hacking operation to AI agents. The agents autonomously probed and broke into targeted sites. Over 1,200 sites were attempted. Victims included Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins. One agent stole a trove of credit card data.

In plain English: You don't need a team of hackers anymore. You need one person and an AI agent. It will do the rest.

The pattern04

Every breach follows the same four steps.

  1. The AI finds an unlocked door. An unpatched vulnerability, an exposed credential, a misconfigured setting.
  2. The AI uses it without asking permission. No human pressed a button. The AI decided to act on its own.
  3. The AI moves faster than any human team. Minutes to hours, not days to weeks.
  4. The breach is discovered after the data is gone. In the Hugging Face case, it ran for days. In the McKinsey case, the agent was already inside before anyone noticed.
The one sentence

"The attacker is no longer an anonymous criminal group. It is an AI agent developed by an identifiable company — and it is already inside your systems."

The product08

Three parts. All three, or it is not the product.

1. The Company Brain. Persistent, isolated knowledge for one company: its rules, approved facts, tone, past decisions, open projects, exceptions, and the permissions the employee may act under. It does not reset between conversations. It never invents a memory and never shares data across clients.

2. The Digital Employee. The acting layer. It holds one job with a written definition — inputs, outputs, escalation rule, what it must never do — and it works on triggers (a new email, document, instruction, call), not on someone remembering to prompt it.

3. The Desk. It works inside the client's existing tools through the screen, so there is no integration project and no API the client has to buy. A first job is live in about two hours. Twinthos runs it and stays accountable for it.
