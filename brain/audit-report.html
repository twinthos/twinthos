The audit.
Three errors.
One reversal.

Five independent reviewers took the block management recommendation apart. Three findings do not survive. Here is what was wrong and what to do instead.

Corrections cited
01
The verdict01

The recommendation was wrong.

Killed

Block management at £5,000 a month. The fee exceeds the buyer's entire pre-tax profit, the incumbent shipped the product two weeks ago, and the law does not say what I said it says.

Survives — for a different reason

Recruitment. Not because of admin volume. Because of profit per employee. One number settles it, and it is on page 9.

The deeper error: I chose the sector by how much admin it had, not by whether the client could afford the fee.

02
Error one02

I priced above their profit.

LineValue
1,000 units × £300 fee£300,000
Pre-tax profit at 10%£30,000
My £2,500/mo fee£30,000/yr

The fee equals 100% of their profit. At the smallest tier. No managing director signs that, ever.

Fees £150–£800/unit/yr (LevyBoard; Wilson Hawkins 2024–25). PBT margin 10–18%, median ~10% (599 UK property-management companies, theaccounts.uk).

04
Error three04

The law does not say that.

I wrote: a managing agent can be the Principal Accountable Person.

The accountable person is normally the freeholder, the RMC, the RTM company or the local authority. A managing agent usually acts on behalf of them.

“You can delegate the work; you cannot delegate the duty.”

The agent produces the evidence. The client signs it off. My "legal defensibility" pitch fails in most leasehold structures — and a competent client would correct me in the first meeting.

The correction07

Ask what the role earns, not what it costs.

A £5,000 monthly fee must be small next to the money the role makes. Not next to the salary. Not next to the admin.

TestRight?
"How much admin do they have?"Wrong
"What does that role cost?"Wrong
"How much profit per head?"Right

Admin volume is a pain measure. Profit per head is a payment measure. Only one of them pays your invoice.

The number that settles it08

£160,200 per recruiter.

Hays, year to 30 June 2025: £972.4m of net fees from 6,070 consultants. That is £160,200 of fee income generated per consultant, per year.

SectorPer head
Recruitment — fees generated£160,200
Block management — profit generated≈ £6,000

A £60,000 annual fee is 37% of one recruiter's output, and 1,000% of one block manager's profit. That is the entire audit in one comparison.

Audit
09
Corrected pricing09

Price the desk, not the person.

The digital employee does not serve one recruiter. It serves a desk — 20 to 30 consultants, all doing the same repeating tasks.

What it coversFee
One workflow, one branch£1,500 pilot
A full desk, 20–30 consultants£5,000/mo
Multi-branch group£1,200/branch

£5,000/mo against 25 consultants is £200 per consultant per month, or roughly 1.5% of the fee income each one generates. That is a defensible invoice.

Direction10

What to do now.

  1. Drop block management. Not forever. Until you have a reference client.
  2. Run the profit-per-head test on any sector before you write another word about it.
  3. Call Hurley and Hipkiss. Both named, both reachable, both in a sector that can afford £5,000.
  4. Sell one desk, not one task. The pilot proves it; the desk pays for it.
  5. Never cite your own pricing file as evidence again. It is a test price, not a fact.
