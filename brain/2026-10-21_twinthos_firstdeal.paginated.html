What you are selling

One job, done by a machine, inside software they already own. Not a platform. Not a transformation. One person’s afternoon back.

Say it like this

“We put a small AI worker on one job in your office — the one nobody wants. It works inside the system you already use. Two weeks to set up, £3,500, and if it doesn’t save the hours we agreed, you don’t pay the second half.”

Three things that make it easy to say yes:

  • It is one job, so there is nothing to rip out.
  • It is £3,500, so one person can sign it.
  • It is two weeks, so nobody has to write a business case.

The call

Five sentences. Say them in order, then stop talking and let them answer.

The script

“Hi, is that the owner? I’ll be quick — I’m not selling software.

You invoice each site monthly and your office manager builds those invoices from timesheets. Is that roughly right?

I build a small AI worker that does that one job — the timesheets, the site sheets, the invoices — inside the system you already use.

It takes two weeks and costs £3,500. If it doesn’t save the hours we agree, you don’t pay the second half.

Can I show you on your own last month’s sheets, Thursday or Friday?”

Write down three things: how many sites, who does the invoicing, and what that person’s week looks like. That is your prep for the second call.

Listen for a number, not for interest. “We do about 140 sites.” “That takes her two days a month.” A number starts a deal. “Interesting” does not.

If they push back. Three answers cover most of it.

They say You say
“We already have software for that.” “Then this sits on top of it. It does the typing between the systems, not the thinking.”
“My office manager would worry.” “Good. She is the one who tells it what to do. It takes the boring half of her day, not her job.”
“How do I know it works?” “You don’t yet. That is what the two weeks are for — it runs on last month’s real sheets.”

If they say no: “What would have to be true for this to be worth twenty minutes?” Write the answer word for word. Ten real objections are worth more than one polite maybe.

The one page

What it is. One worker that does your site paperwork: collects the timesheets, checks them against the site sheet, builds the monthly invoice, and lists what is missing.

What goes in. The timesheets and site sheets you already have. Nothing moves. Nothing gets replaced.

What comes out. A clean invoice pack per site, a list of gaps before they become disputes, and a short weekly note of what was done.

What it must never do. It does not approve hours, sign anything, or change a price. Anything that looks like a dispute goes to a named person and waits.

The two-week test. We run it on last month’s sheets. If it doesn’t match what your team produced, you keep your money.

The price. £3,500 for the install and the test. £1,250 a month after that, cancel with a month’s notice.

The week

Day Do this
Monday Send ten emails at 7am. Call the same ten at 10am.
Tuesday Same ten again, different time of day.
Wednesday Send the one page to anyone who gave you a number.
Thursday Two demos on their own files.
Friday One follow-up per person, then stop.

Ten a day, five days. Fifty conversations is enough to find the first yes. If it is not, the job is wrong, not the market — change the job and keep the list.

What done looks like

One paid install at £3,500, a start date in the diary, and the job written down in one paragraph that both of you have signed.
