AI is cutting the junior roles first. Who trains your next senior hire?

In the United States, employment for software developers aged 22 to 25 fell nearly 20 percent between 2024 and 2025, while older developers kept growing. The first rungs of the career ladder are the ones being cut.

Stanford HAI reports that employment among US software developers aged 22 to 25 fell nearly 20 percent since 2024, while headcount for older developers continued to grow. The report says AI's labour-market effects are concentrated in hiring pipelines and the youngest workers in exposed occupations.

In this guide

    What the data shows

    Stanford HAI reports that employment among software developers aged 22 to 25 has fallen nearly 20 percent since 2024, even as their older colleagues' headcount grew [1]. The report says AI's labour-market effects are concentrated in hiring pipelines and the youngest workers in exposed occupations [1].

    The fall is measured in one occupation and one country. It does not prove the same pattern holds in other jobs or other places, and the report does not claim that it does.

    Why the first rung matters most

    Entry-level roles do two jobs at once. They do routine work, and they teach the judgement that later senior work depends on. If AI takes the routine work, the training that came with it can disappear too.

    Stanford reports that productivity gains are largest in structured, measurable tasks, with smaller gains where deeper reasoning is needed. It also notes that recent evidence suggests heavy reliance on AI may carry long-term learning penalties that slow skill development [2].

    What the evidence does not settle

    The open question is whether firms will rebuild the first rung in a different form, through supervised practice, structured assessment or new kinds of entry roles, or whether they will simply hire fewer people and wait for experienced staff to be available.

    OUR READING: firms that treat AI as a reason to stop hiring early-career people are trading a short-term saving for a shortage of experienced staff in five to ten years. Firms that redesign the entry role around supervision and judgement keep the ladder intact.

    Sources

    1. Stanford HAI, "Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report"
    2. Stanford HAI, The 2026 AI Index Report, Economy chapter (PDF)
