What answering your phone with software really costs

The model can cost pennies. But what does it take to answer a real customer, check the diary, know when to transfer and leave a proper record?

The missed call isn't the whole problem. It's the promised callback that slips, the booking that was never confirmed and the customer who has to explain themselves twice.

The short answer

At Google's introductory October 2026 prices, a hypothetical batch of 600 text enquiries uses about $25.40 of model input and output tokens. That is not the cost of an operating telephone service: speech, telephone minutes, integrations, oversight and support are additional.

The model costs less than a coffee a day

On 13 October 2026 Google's Gemini 4 Argon became generally available at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output. A token is about three quarters of a word, so $2 buys roughly 750,000 words of input. That pricing covers model tokens. Voice transcription, speech generation, telephone infrastructure and customer-system integration require separate budgeting.

For a simplified text-only calculation, a short enquiry with 4,000 input and 800 output tokens costs about $0.016. A longer one at 20,000 input and 3,000 output tokens costs $0.070. A hypothetical monthly usage mix of 7.2 million input and 1.1 million output tokens totals $25.40 in model fees alone. Read the word introductory. Google has said the price doubles on 1 December 2026, so plan for about $51 a month from then. Even at double, the model is the smallest line in the budget.

That is the part that got cheap. Nothing else did.

The bill that never arrives

The expensive part of a phone is the call nobody answered. Someone wanted a price, a booking, an answer your website could not give. When it rang out, most did not ring back. They rang the next name on the list.

Then the version that stings, because this one was yours. You did answer, on a ladder or in a van, and said you would call them back. The day happened and the promise quietly rotted.

Whoever sits nearest the phone carries that. Their own work moves to seven in the evening, and no invoice ever shows what it cost.

What actually breaks, and what fixing it costs

Cheap answers are worthless if they are confidently wrong. Four jobs sit around the model, and none of them got cheaper with it.

Wiring. The model does not know your diary, your inbox, or who is on a payment plan. Someone connects it. Rules. What counts as a valid refund, which quotes need approval. That lives in your colleagues' heads, not your documents. Review. Right 95% of the time is still a liability if the 5% is expensive.

Then the line. Below it the software acts alone, above it a person decides, with logging and one switch that turns the whole thing off.

Why most of these projects stall

MIT's NANDA report, published in August 2025, found roughly 95% of generative AI pilots never reached measurable profit-and-loss impact. It blamed integration, data readiness and the workflow itself. Not model quality, and never price.

So the pattern to plan around is not the model. It is whether the thing knows your rules and stops where it should. That work is weeks, not minutes, and it is the same work whether the tokens cost $2 or $20.

Being early is still worth something. Not because the model is scarce, since every competitor can buy it at the same price. Early buys you the exceptions list, found while nobody is watching.

The limits worth knowing before you buy

The Remote Labour Index is a 2026 benchmark of 240 real freelance projects, run by the Center for AI Safety and Scale AI. It found the best agent finishing 2.5% of projects end to end. Major models scored between 0.8% and 2.1%.

Given real briefs and deadlines, they failed on the long tail. That means talking to the client, working through feedback, and delivering something a person would accept and pay for.

Read that the useful way round. It is a good reason to let software answer, log and chase. It is a good reason to keep pricing decisions, complaints and anything with a deadline you cannot miss behind a person.

What this means for you

Start with one line: the number your customers actually ring. Point software at it for a month with someone reviewing every call. Count what it got right, what it escalated and what it missed. If it holds, widen it. If it does not, you have spent a month and a few dollars rather than a customer list.

What this means for you

The model is now the cheapest part of the job. Answering, logging and chasing 600 enquiries a month costs about $25 today and about $51 once Google doubles the price on 1 December 2026. What costs real money is the wiring, the rules and the review that make it safe to leave alone. Decide what it may never do without a person, and write that down before you switch it on.

Questions people actually ask

So what does answering my phone cost in a month?

At the introductory price, about $25.40 for 600 mixed enquiries, which works out under a dollar a day. Google doubles the price on 1 December 2026, which takes it to about $51. Both figures cover the model only, not the setup or the review.

What breaks first?

The wiring and the rules, not the model. It does not know your diary, your refund policy or which quotes need approval until someone connects it and teaches it.

What happens when it gets something wrong?

Every workflow gets a line drawn before it is switched on. Below the line it acts alone, above it a person decides, and everything is logged with the reason.
