The real cost of running an AI employee vs hiring one, in 2026 numbers

A worked example with public list prices, published wages, and the four assumptions that change the answer. The cost is rarely the headline number.

By Twinthos ResearchPublished 14 October 20267 sources, all linked

The short version

  • On list price alone, a top model call costs less than a minute of an entry-level employee's time on a thousand tasks a day. The headline comparison is misleading.
  • The real comparison needs the model's effective cost per task, not its list price. Effective cost divides the list price by the share of calls that finish the task without a person stepping in. A model that is 60% reliable costs 1.67x its list price per successful task.
  • For a small team, the worked example in this article puts the monthly all-in cost of an AI workflow that handles roughly 1,000 repetitive enquiries at between £1,800 and £2,200, depending on model, reliability, and how much of the work is human review.
  • A junior hire in the UK doing the same work costs £1,800 to £2,400 a month fully loaded, with paid leave, pension and the right to switch off. The comparison depends on whether the work is growing, falling, or steady.

The cheapest version of an AI employee is a top model on a public API. The most expensive version is a top model plus a person who checks its work. The two numbers are not the same, and most published comparisons use the cheap one.

What the headline numbers look like

Public list prices in October 2026. These are the numbers on the price pages; they do not include the build, the integration, the monitoring, or the human review.

  • Claude Sonnet 4.5 (Anthropic). $3 per million input tokens, $15 per million output tokens.[1]
  • Claude Haiku 4.5 (Anthropic). $1 per million input tokens, $5 per million output tokens.[1]
  • Claude Opus 4.1 (Anthropic). $15 per million input tokens, $75 per million output tokens.[1]
  • GPT-5 (OpenAI). $1.25 / $10 per million tokens for the standard tier; $3 / $22 for the pro tier.[2]
  • Gemini 2.5 Pro (Google). $1.25 / $10 per million tokens.[3]
  • Gemini 2.0 Flash (Google). $0.10 / $0.40 per million tokens.[3]

At list price, a Sonnet-class call to handle a 500-token customer enquiry with a 200-token answer costs about $0.0045, or less than half a US cent. A thousand of them is $4.50. The same thousand enquiries, handled by a junior employee, costs roughly £450 a day fully loaded in the UK, or about $570 at October 2026 exchange rates. The headline comparison favours the model by two orders of magnitude.

The headline is not the answer.

The four assumptions that change the answer

Four inputs move the comparison by an order of magnitude.

  • Reliability on the real task. A model that succeeds 80% of the time is not a substitute for an employee. It is a tool that needs a person for the other 20%. The effective cost per successful task is list price divided by the success rate. At 60% success, a $0.0045 call is $0.0075 per successful task; at 40% it is $0.011. The model is the same. The work it takes to be reliable is different.
  • Prompt size. A customer-service workflow with retrieval needs more input tokens than a 500-token customer message. A coding workflow with repository context can be 50,000 input tokens. A single Sonnet 4.5 call with 50,000 input tokens and 5,000 output tokens is $0.225, not $0.0045. The cost grows with context, not with messages.
  • Human review. If 20% of calls need a person to check, the cost of the person is real. A junior reviewer spending ten minutes a day on 200 calls a day spends 200 minutes, or about £30 a day in loaded time. That is more than the model cost of 1,000 calls.
  • Integration and observability. The build, the auth, the rate limits, the audit log, the rollback, the on-call. The NANDA report and BCG's 2024 survey both put integration at roughly twice the cost of the model in a production deployment.[4][5]

A worked example for a small team

A UK small business that gets 1,000 customer enquiries a month, each answerable in a 500-token prompt with a 200-token response. The numbers below use public list prices and HMRC-published thresholds.

  • 1,000 enquiries times 0.7 input + 0.2 output (in dollars per million tokens): at Sonnet 4.5 list price, the model cost is about $4.50 a month. At Opus 4.1 it is about $19.50. At Gemini 2.5 Pro it is about $1.50.
  • Reliability: 70%. Effective cost per successful task is list price / 0.7. For Sonnet 4.5, the effective cost is about $6.50 a month.
  • Reviewer time for the 30%. 300 calls a month, ten minutes each, at a loaded £30 an hour: 50 hours, or £1,500 a month.
  • Integration amortised. A small business deploying a customer-service workflow with a real CRM, an audit log, a rollback and a person on call: the build is roughly £8,000 to £15,000, amortised over two years: £330 to £625 a month.
  • Total. Between £1,830 a month (Sonnet 4.5, light integration) and £2,150 a month (Opus 4.1, fuller integration). The model is a small part of the bill.

A junior hire doing the same work full-time, at the October 2026 UK National Living Wage plus employer National Insurance and auto-enrolment pension: about £1,900 a month, with paid leave and the right to switch off.[6][7] The two numbers are within £200 of each other. The decision is not about cost.

What the numbers do not capture

The cost comparison misses four things.

  • Variability. The AI workflow handles 1,000 or 10,000 enquiries a month for the same integration cost. A junior hire handles about 160 hours of work, no matter how many enquiries arrive. If volume is growing, the AI workflow is cheaper per unit; if volume is steady, the comparison is closer than it looks.
  • Latency. A junior employee works eight hours a day. The AI workflow answers at 3 a.m. and does not take leave. Some of the work is at night, on weekends, and across time zones; some of it is not.
  • Judgment. An employee will pick up that an enquiry is actually a complaint. A model that is 70% reliable will not. The hard cases go to the person, and the easy cases go to the model. The split is the design.
  • Error cost. A wrong answer to a customer costs more than the call. A refund that is too generous, a delivery date that is wrong, an apology that is mis-spelled. The model's reliability has to be measured on the work that matters, not on the average.

A short checklist before you sign the cheque

Before replacing any human work with a model, run the checklist.

  1. What is the volume? If it grows, the model is the cheaper per-unit option. If it does not, the comparison is closer.
  2. What is the reliability on the real task? Measure 200 calls, not 20. Reliability on the easy 80% does not generalise.
  3. What is the human review cost? If the review is more than the saving, the deployment has not earned its place.
  4. What is the integration cost? Build it once. The NANDA report and BCG's 2024 survey put it at twice the model cost.
  5. What is the error cost? A wrong answer to a customer can be more expensive than the call. Design for the hard case, not the average.

The cost of running an AI workflow is rarely the list price. It is the list price divided by reliability, plus the human review, plus the integration. The decision is rarely about cost. It is about volume, judgment, and the kind of work the business wants to scale.

Common questions

What does it cost to run an AI workflow in 2026?

On list price alone, a top model is less than half a US cent per 500-token call. The effective cost is list price divided by the model's reliability on the real task. A 1,000-enquiry-a-month workflow with 70% reliability and human review on the 30% is between £1,800 and £2,200 a month for a small UK business, including integration.

Is an AI employee cheaper than a junior hire?

The all-in cost of an AI workflow that handles 1,000 enquiries a month and a junior hire doing the same work in the UK in October 2026 are within £200 of each other. The decision is not about cost. It is about volume, judgment and the kind of work the business wants to scale.

Which model should a small team use?

There is no single answer. Claude Sonnet 4.5 is the workhorse tier at Anthropic, balancing capability and cost. Claude Haiku 4.5 is cheaper and faster for short prompts. Claude Opus 4.1 is the top tier for hard tasks. Gemini 2.0 Flash is the cheapest of the credible options for simple, high-volume work. Measure on your own data.

What is the biggest hidden cost?

Integration, in the NANDA 2025 and BCG 2024 reports. Build, auth, audit, rollback and the on-call person. The model is the cheap part. The integration is not.
