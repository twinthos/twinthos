88 percent of organisations use AI. Almost none run agents at scale.

The Stanford AI Index 2026 shows fast adoption and a much slower move to autonomous agents. The gap between the two is where most of the money and the risk now sits.

In 2025, 88 percent of surveyed organisations used AI in at least one business function, up from 78 percent. Agent deployment, where AI carries out multi-step work on its own, was in single digits in nearly all business functions.

In this guide

    Adoption is near-universal

    Organisational AI use reached 88 percent of surveyed organisations in 2025 [1]. Generative AI is used in at least one business function at 70 percent of organisations, and China and Europe posted the highest year-on-year increases [1].

    Generative AI reached 53 percent of the population within three years, faster than the personal computer or the internet did. Adoption varies by country and tracks income closely. The United States ranks 24th, at 28.3 percent [2].

    Agents are not in most businesses yet

    The same report finds that AI agent use is at an early stage. Across most business functions, a majority of respondents reported no agent use at all. Scaled use is in the single digits for nearly all functions [1].

    Stanford reports that agent deployment was in the single digits across nearly all business functions, and that a majority of respondents reported no agent use at all in most functions [1]. Stanford also notes that gains are smaller in tasks needing deeper reasoning [1].

    Where the measured gains are

    Studies report productivity gains of 14 to 15 percent in customer support, 26 percent in software development and 50 percent in marketing output [2]. The gains are largest in structured, measurable work where outputs are easy to check. They are smaller in tasks that need deeper judgement [2].

    The report also raises a caution that is easy to skip: recent evidence suggests heavy reliance on AI may slow the development of skills over time [2].

    What it means

    OUR READING: most firms are past the question of whether to try AI and stuck on the question of who runs it once it is working. The companies that move ahead will be the ones that write down which work an AI system may do alone, which work needs a person, and how success will be measured.
