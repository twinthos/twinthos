What is a digital employee, and how is it different from a chatbot?

Both use the same underlying models. The difference is who initiates the work, where the work happens, and who is accountable when something goes wrong.

The short answer: a chatbot waits for someone to ask it a question. A digital employee is given a job, has its own computer, inbox and tools, and runs the job inside written rules with one named person responsible.

The two products, side by side

AI chatbot (ChatGPT, Grok, Gemini)Digital employee (Twinthos)
Who starts the workThe user, by typing a promptThe work itself: an invoice arrives, a lead comes in, a record changes
Where it runsBrowser tab, on the user's machineIts own cloud computer with its own inbox, phone, memory and team-chat seats
Where the actions landChat window, draft document the user copiesInside Xero, HubSpot, Gmail, Slack, your other tools, via API
HoursWhen the user is at the keyboard24/7, including nights and bank holidays
ApprovalUser reads and decidesWritten rules; stops and asks the agreed person at the agreed line
Accountable humanNone — the user is responsible for what they do with the outputOne named operator at Twinthos, named in writing

The two-minute test

Ask the product: does it have its own inbox? Does it write into your tools without you copying and pasting? Does it run while you sleep? Does it stop at an agreed line and ask a person? If the answer to all four is yes, you are looking at a digital employee. If the answer to any of them is no, you are looking at a chatbot, however good.

Why the difference matters for a UK SMB

A chatbot increases what one person can do in a day. A digital employee takes a recurring, defined job off the team's plate entirely. The first is a force multiplier for the owner's hours. The second changes the shape of the owner's week. For a 12-person business losing 11 hours a week to admin (Amex SME Barometer, July 2026), only the second one closes the gap.
