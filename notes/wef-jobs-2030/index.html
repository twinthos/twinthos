170 million jobs created, 92 million displaced. The net figure hides the sorting.

The World Economic Forum projects a net gain of 78 million jobs by 2030. The headline is reassuring. The detail is about which jobs grow, which shrink and which skills change.

The World Economic Forum projects 170 million new jobs and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net gain of 78 million. Job disruption will equal 22 percent of jobs by 2030. Nearly 40 percent of the skills required on the job are set to change.

In this guide

    The headline numbers

    The Future of Jobs Report 2025 projects 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030 [1]. That nets to 78 million more jobs [1][2].

    The gross movement is much larger than the net. The report says job disruption will equal 22 percent of jobs by 2030 [1]. The data draws on more than 1,000 companies [1].

    Why the net figure misleads

    A net gain of 78 million is an average across the world. It says nothing about who moves from a shrinking role to a growing one. A cashier and a data analyst can both be counted in the same total without either one changing jobs.

    The report finds nearly 40 percent of skills required on the job are set to change by 2030 [1]. For most workers, the question is not whether the job exists, but whether their current skills still match what it needs.

    What to check in your own sector

    Three questions separate a useful forecast from a comforting one. Which tasks in a given role are repeatable and measurable? Which of those tasks are already done by software? And what training or hiring route leads into the roles that are growing?

    OUR READING: the sectors most exposed are the ones with large volumes of routine, rule-based work. The sectors that gain are the ones that need people to decide, supervise and handle exceptions.
