170 million jobs created, 92 million displaced by 2030

The World Economic Forum's 2025 report is a net-positive headline with a hard detail underneath: most of the people who need new skills may not get them.

By Twinthos ResearchPublished 8 October 2026Updated 13 October 20262 sources, all linked

The short version

  • The WEF projects 170 million new jobs and 92 million displaced by 2030, a net gain of 78 million. Disruption touches 22% of jobs.
  • Of every 100 workers, 59 are projected to need reskilling or upskilling by 2030, and 11 of them are unlikely to receive it.
  • Cashiers and administrative assistants remain among the fastest-declining roles. Graphic designers join them as generative AI spreads.
  • These are employer expectations from a survey of over 1,000 companies, not a forecast model with a guarantee.

The headline from the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 is reassuring: more jobs are created than lost. The detail is the part worth reading, because it shows where the cost of change falls.[1],[2]

The headline numbers

Drawing on data from over 1,000 companies, the WEF projects that technology, demographic shifts, geoeconomic tension and economic pressure will together create 170 million new roles by 2030 and displace 92 million. The net is an increase of 78 million jobs. The WEF describes the total churn as equal to 22% of jobs.[1]

Where the growth and the losses are

The largest growth in absolute terms is in frontline roles: farmworkers, delivery drivers and construction workers. Care jobs such as nursing and education roles such as secondary school teaching also grow, driven by demographics. Specialist roles in AI, robotics and renewable energy grow quickly from a smaller base.[1]

On the decline side, cashiers and administrative assistants remain among the fastest-declining roles. They are now joined by graphic designers, which the WEF attributes to generative AI.[1] Price pressures and slower growth are projected to displace a further 6 million jobs globally by 2030.[1]

The harder number: who gets retrained

The WEF cites the skills gap as the most significant barrier to business transformation, named by 63% of employers. Nearly 40% of the skills needed on the job are set to change. Then comes the figure that matters most:[1]

The net number is positive. The risk is concentrated in the people who do not get retrained.

What employers say they will do

Upskilling is the most common planned response. Cutting staff is planned by a large minority. These are not alternatives: the same employer can do both, and the survey does not say how the numbers overlap.

What the report does not say

  • It is not a forecast with a track record. It is employers' expectations, collected by survey. Plans change when conditions change.
  • Net numbers hide individuals. A net gain of 78 million jobs says nothing about whether the person whose role is displaced can take one of the new ones.
  • It is global. Growth in farm, care and delivery roles reflects demographics and cost of living as much as technology. Local labour markets will differ.
  • It does not isolate AI. The 170 and 92 million figures bundle several forces. The AI-specific pieces are the skills and employer-plan numbers above.
Our reading

For a small business, the most useful line is the plainest: administrative assistants are among the fastest-declining roles. That describes tasks, not people. Scheduling, data entry and document handling are the work automation takes first, and the person who used to do them is the one most worth retraining into something that needs judgment.

Common questions

How many jobs will AI create or displace by 2030?

The WEF's 2025 report does not give an AI-only figure. It projects 170 million new roles and 92 million displaced by 2030 from all drivers combined, including technology, demographics and economic pressures. AI-specific findings include that 41% of employers plan to reduce staff where AI can automate tasks.

What is the net effect on jobs?

A net gain of 78 million jobs by 2030, according to the World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025. Job disruption in total equates to 22% of jobs.

Which jobs are declining fastest?

The WEF names cashiers and administrative assistants among the fastest-declining roles, now joined by graphic designers as generative AI spreads.

How many workers need retraining?

The WEF estimates that 59 of every 100 workers will need reskilling or upskilling by 2030, and that 11 of those 100 are unlikely to receive it, over 120 million workers at medium-term risk of redundancy.
