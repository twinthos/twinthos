Small business owners lose 11 hours a week to admin. Here is what that costs.

A July 2026 American Express survey of 1,000 UK business owners found admin takes about six working days a month. Sales gets 3.6. Here is the maths on what that costs, and how much to trust it.

The short version

  • UK owners estimate they spend 11 hours a week on admin and finance tasks. That is about six working days a month.
  • Sales and business development gets 3.6 days a month. Admin takes roughly 1.7 times as long.
  • At 11 hours, a year of admin is 572 hours, around 72 eight-hour days. The price depends on what an hour of your time is worth.
  • The figures are self-reported estimates from a card company's survey. Treat them as direction, not measurement.

In July 2026, American Express and Small Business Saturday UK published their annual SME Business Barometer. It surveyed 1,000 owners of micro, small and medium-sized businesses in the UK. One number led the release: owners estimate they spend an average of 11 hours a week on administrative or finance-related tasks.[1]

That is nearly a day and a half every week, before any customer work. This guide sets out what the survey found, does the arithmetic on what it costs, and says plainly how much weight the number can bear.

What the survey found

  • 11 ha week on admin and finance, on average
  • 3.6working days a month on sales and growth
  • 54%say paperwork gets in the way of running the business
  • 36%name their own lack of capacity as the biggest barrier to growth

The survey also found that one in five owners (20%) work 60 hours or more a week.[1] A second report, in This is Money, says the fieldwork was done by Opinium for American Express, and that 54% of owners at firms with fewer than 250 staff say paperwork gets in the way of running their business.[2] The release puts the admin figure at "approximately six working days per month", and the sales and business development figure at "just over half that time".

What 11 hours a week costs in a year

The conversion is simple. Eleven hours a week over 52 weeks is 572 hours. At eight hours a day that is about 72 working days a year, roughly 14 five-day working weeks a year spent on tasks that do not, by themselves, bring in revenue.

The cost depends on one input the survey cannot know: what an hour of your time is worth. For a founder, that is the margin on the work they would otherwise be doing. For an employee doing the admin, it is their loaded cost per hour. Put your own figures in:

Why admin wins the week

Admin is the work that cannot be skipped and cannot be scheduled for later without a penalty. An unsent invoice delays cash. An unanswered enquiry goes to a competitor. A missing document stalls a job. None of it is hard. All of it needs someone to remember to push it forward.

That pattern shows up in the other numbers. When 36% of owners say their own capacity is the biggest barrier to growth, they are describing the same constraint from the other side: the owner's hours are the bottleneck, and admin is spending them.[1]

None of it is hard. All of it needs someone to remember to push it forward.

How far to trust the number

Four things to keep in mind before quoting it.

  • It is an estimate. Respondents were asked to estimate their own time. People are poor at estimating recurring small tasks, and could be wrong in either direction.[1]
  • It is an average. A sole trader with a few clients and a 40-person business with a finance function sit under the same headline. Your number may be half or double.
  • The sponsor sells business cards. That does not make the figure wrong. It does mean the framing, "time pressure", suits the sponsor's message about tools that save time.
  • It is UK-only. The survey covers UK owners, so we do not stretch it to other countries.

Where owners say AI could help

The same survey asked about technology. Among owners who want to delegate work, 33% said they would use generative AI tools for general administration and 23% for finance and accounting. One in five (21%) said AI is like having another member of staff, and 41% of those already using it use it for generating or editing content.[1]

These are stated intentions and early habits, not measured results. The more useful signal is what the owner is trying to delegate: routine, repeatable, rule-bound tasks, not judgment calls.

A way to find your own number

Our reading

The survey average is a starting point. The useful number is yours, and it takes two weeks to get.

  1. Log for ten working days. Each time you switch to something that is not customer work or sales, write the task and a rough duration. A note on your phone is enough.
  2. Sort the log into three groups. Tasks that repeat on a schedule (invoicing, payroll prep, chasing). Tasks triggered by an event (a new enquiry, a booking change). Tasks that need your judgment.
  3. Price the first two groups. Multiply weekly hours by 52 and by your hourly value, as in the calculator above.
  4. Keep the third group. Judgment is the part worth your hours. The first two groups are where hours are most likely to come back.

Common questions

How many hours a week do small business owners spend on admin?

An average of 11 hours a week on administrative or finance-related tasks, according to the American Express SME Business Barometer published in July 2026. The survey covered 1,000 UK micro, small and medium-sized business owners. The figure is self-estimated.

How do I work out what admin costs my business?

Multiply weekly admin hours by 52, then by the value of an hour of the time involved. For an owner, use the margin earned by the work they would otherwise do. For an employee, use their fully loaded hourly cost. The calculator in this guide does the arithmetic.

Is 11 hours a week typical?

It is the average in one survey. Individual businesses vary widely with size, sector and how much of the admin is already handled by staff, an accountant or software. Logging your own week for two weeks gives a better estimate.

Which admin tasks suit automation first?

Tasks that repeat on a schedule or start from a clear trigger, with fixed steps and a clear finish: invoice reminders, booking confirmations, document collection, routine data entry. Tasks that need judgment are better left with a person.
