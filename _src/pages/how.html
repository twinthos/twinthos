02 / THE ARCHITECTURE

One operator.
Specialist hands.

The coordinating agent receives your instruction and chooses from the tools and delegated tasks permitted for its job. Specialists can help with research, sales follow-up, content or support where appropriate.

A dedicated computer and workspace give the work continuity. The business knowledge store contains relevant rules, context and past work; the agent shouldn't need you to repeat everything each morning.

The design can include a dedicated inbox, messaging channels, a phone integration and an approved card/payment route—but none of those should bypass access or spending controls.

01 / COMMAND

Send the instruction.

Communicate through an agreed channel, such as email, Telegram or Slack, after identity and access are configured.

02 / EXECUTION

Work across tools.

The agent follows its job specification, uses approved applications and keeps track of pending steps.

03 / OVERSIGHT

Approve exceptions.

Requests beyond approved rules are escalated, including spending, discounts, disputes or sensitive operations.

03 / KEEPING THE CONTEXT

Real memory is
business memory.

Long-term memory means maintained knowledge: voice examples, customer context, offers, procedures and the record of what happened last time.

It is not perfect recall. Your information needs agreed access, correction, retention and deletion rules. A good system asks when context is missing or uncertain.

That continuity is what turns individual prompts into a recurring role.

04 / DELIVERY

From first conversation
to a managed role.

01 / ASSESS

Find the actual bottleneck.

Measure the repetitive steps and where human attention is consumed. Choose one job worth addressing.

02 / SPECIFY

Write the job.

Agree the tools, permissions, escalation rules, metrics, communication channels and expected volume.

03 / TEST

Prove the normal and abnormal.

Verify sample tasks, provider failures, strange inputs, approval requirements and recovery before real operation.

04 / OPERATE

Stay accountable.

Keep the integrations working, review summaries, update the rules and report on the agreed work.

The power is
in the boundary.

We do not give an agent permission to do everything simply because it can use tools. The role is constrained, high-impact actions are reviewed and outcomes are assessed against the work actually done.

The public Sentinel project demonstrates a narrow read-only execution boundary; it is not a claim that all Twinthos integrations are automatically secured by that prototype.
