What owners say

The questions they asked first.

Every owner who signs up asks something similar. Here are the questions, the honest answers, and what happened next.

"Is this really an employee?"

The question: "It is software, not a person. We call it a digital employee because it is given a role, works through its own computer, phone and email, and carries out agreed responsibilities. You decide what it may do. Anything outside the agreed rules goes to your team."

What happened next: The owner defined one job and watched it work for two weeks before adding a second.

"Will this replace my staff?"

The question: "No. It takes the repeat follow-through off them. Decisions, relationships and judgement stay with your people."

What happened next: The team stopped doing invoice chasing and started doing the work that brings in revenue.

"What if it gets something wrong?"

The question: "Work is tested before launch and monitored while it runs. Your team reviews and approves what needs approval, and we fix and refine the procedure when something is off."

What happened next: One wrong reply in week two. The procedure was updated within the day. No client noticed.

"Why not just use ChatGPT?"

The question: "ChatGPT gives you answers in a chat window. Twinthos gives you a digital employee that does the work: its own computer, inbox and phone, working inside your tools, following your procedures and reporting what it did. We set it up, monitor it and improve it for you."

What happened next: The owner tried ChatGPT for a month. Replies sat in a tab. With Twinthos, they sent themselves.

"Which tools can it work with?"

The question: "The systems and permissions assigned to its role. We confirm what connects, and test it, before anything is promised. A tool being technically connectable does not mean it is connected for you."

What happened next: The proposal listed exactly what connects: Gmail, HubSpot, Xero and Google Drive. Nothing else was promised.

"What does £3,500 per month cover?"

The question: "Designing and building one agreed workflow, connecting the agreed tools, testing, watching and maintaining it, agreed rule changes, and reporting and support. Your proposal lists the exact scope and any software that is charged separately."

What happened next: The owner compared the monthly fee to the admin hours recovered. The break-even point was week five.

"What is the minimum term?"

The question: "Three months, so £10,500 in service fees. Billing starts when your workflow is live. After that, either side can end it with 30 days' written notice. Prices exclude VAT where applicable."

What happened next: The audit was credited against the first month. The owner signed after week two of the pilot.

"Is the audit fee credited?"

The question: "If you go ahead with the managed service within 30 days of receiving the audit, the £999 is credited in full against your first month."

What happened next: The owner proceeded within the 30-day window. The audit cost nothing.

"Will this give my team more to manage?"

The question: "Your team helps define the process, gives the agreed access and makes the decisions above the line. We build and run the rest. Any review work left for your team is counted when we judge whether it is worth it."

What happened next: The team spent less time on admin and more time on the work that needed their judgement.
