Understand it.
Before you trust it.

The exciting part is what an operator could do. The important part is knowing its role, limits, cost and what happens when something goes wrong.

What it is.

Eight useful components. One clearly defined responsibility.
What makes this different from a chatbot?+

A chatbot answers questions. Twinthos is a managed role: an orchestrator with a persistent working environment, tools, written instructions and context that can carry tasks across sessions. Its authority is still limited.

Does it really get its own computer, inbox and number?+

A dedicated workspace and assigned communication channels are parts of the design. Email, messaging and phone integrations depend on the agreed job, provider availability and tested permissions.

What about its own card?+

A dedicated card or purchasing route can be configured for a scoped role where appropriate. It is not permission to spend freely: the payment provider, merchant limits, approval process and who holds financial responsibility must be defined before use.

Does it have long-term memory?+

Yes, the system can use a persistent knowledge store for client context, playbooks, tone examples and prior decisions. That memory is maintained and access-controlled; it is not guaranteed perfect recall or automatic retention of everything said.

Can I instruct it through Telegram or Slack?+

Connected messaging channels can be configured, including Telegram and Slack. The specific channel, user identity checks and what instructions may trigger actions are agreed per deployment.

Can it work at night?+

Scheduled software workflows can continue outside business hours. Provider uptime, alert delivery, manual approvals and Twinthos human support are separate from 24/7 software availability.

The business terms.

No trick tiers. Clear boundaries.

What is the £999 audit?+

A one-off assessment and written plan for recurring work. It does not deploy an employee, and you keep the plan.

What does £5,000 a month buy?+

Design, implementation, testing and management of one agreed workflow. The specific tool access, volume, changes, reporting and software usage are written into your proposal.

How long is the minimum term?+

Three months (£15,000 in service fees), with billing starting at launch, then 30 days' written notice as described in your agreement. Fees exclude VAT where applicable.

Is the £999 audit credited?+

Yes, in full against the first managed month when you proceed within 30 days of receiving the audit.

Will it replace my team or guarantee results?+

No guaranteed savings or sales. The point is to take repetitive follow-through off people while they retain customer relationships, judgement and decision-making authority.

Who stays in charge?

What does it ask permission for?+

Anything outside the agreed job: unapproved spending, price changes, sensitive customer matters, new tool permissions, disputes and decisions reserved for staff.

Where is my information stored?+

Providers and hosting arrangements differ. They are documented, along with model use, data retention, access rights and deletion procedures, before the workflow launches.

What if it fails?+

The plan defines how to pause work, detect problems, notify the responsible person and recover. Monitoring reduces blind spots; it doesn't make failure impossible.
