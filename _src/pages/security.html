FIVE QUESTIONS TO ANSWER BEFORE LAUNCH

01 / ACCESS

What can it touch?

Which accounts, applications, files and actions are required—and how is access revoked?

02 / MEMORY

What does it keep?

Which business facts, conversations and corrections persist, where they live, who may read them and how they are corrected or deleted.

03 / PAYMENTS

Can it spend?

If a dedicated card or payment route is part of the job, define provider controls, limits, authorised merchants and a human approval process for consequential spending.

04 / EVIDENCE

What happened?

Define audit records, status updates, approval history, retention and what can be exported for review.

05 / RESPONSIBILITY

Who takes over?

Agree incident response, failures, handoffs, service endings and removal of access or retained data.

06 / PROVIDERS

Where is data processed?

List the model and software providers, hosting locations, cross-border processing and relevant privacy settings for the actual installation.

The card is a tool.
Not a blank cheque.

Where a card or purchasing route is appropriate, it is configured for the agreed purpose and controlled by provider limits and human authorisation. Simply giving software payment credentials is not a safe spending policy.

Long-term memory is treated the same way: useful context, with boundaries around collection, access, correction, retention and deletion. The plan must be specific to your organisation.

OPEN ENGINEERING

Show the test.
